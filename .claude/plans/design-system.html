Hi-fi design system · v1.0 · 5 Nov 2026

Dave Portfolio

A black-and-white system with a single gradient. Pages are white, type is light and precise, and every control is black. Colour appears only as a soft gradient glow behind a few key moments. Frosted, translucent surfaces sit over that glow and give the page depth without adding decoration. The result should feel calm, premium and easy to trust: the qualities a client looks for in an assistant.

Look

  • White canvas, black type, black controls
  • Cool neutral greys for structure and quiet text
  • One gradient, used as light rather than paint
  • Frosted surfaces with a hairline edge
  • Small corners and short, round-ended buttons

Feel

  • Calm and spacious, with one loud moment per page
  • Thin, precise display type at large sizes
  • Sections told apart by texture, not by colour
  • Motion that is slow, small and easy to ignore

Function

  • One filled button for the main action on each screen
  • Every control reachable by keyboard and at least 44px to tap
  • All text at 4.5:1 contrast or better, in light and dark
  • Frosted glass falls back to solid surfaces when asked
Principles

Four rules for every page

When a design decision isn't covered elsewhere on this page, these four rules settle it.

Quiet, not empty

The work is the subject. Each page has one designed moment at the top. Every section after it is short, plain and leads somewhere: to a fuller page, to a piece of work, or to booking a call. Remove decoration before adding it.

Glass earns its place

A frosted surface only goes where there is colour or texture behind it to blur, and nothing animating inside it. A page holds about six blurred elements at most. Anything else sits on a solid surface.

Size and weight carry hierarchy

Important text gets bigger or heavier; it never gets paler. Quiet text has a floor of 4.5:1 contrast, so the gap between headings and body comes from scale, weight and space.

One action per screen

Each screen has a single filled black button for its main action, such as booking a call. Everything else is a light pill link, a text link or a tile, so the main action is never in doubt.

Colour

Black, white and one gradient

Everything a visitor reads or presses is black, white or a cool neutral grey. The gradient is the only colour in the system. It behaves like light falling on the page: a soft glow behind the hero, small marks beside labels, the frame of a playing video and one cover in a stack of documents. It never sits behind text and never fills a button. Values on this page update when you switch theme or gradient.

How colour is used

  • Black means "do this"Filled black is reserved for the main action and for selections the visitor has made. Nothing decorative is filled black except the closing band.
  • Greys build structureRules, borders, grouped fills and quiet text are all neutral greys. They should never carry meaning on their own.
  • The gradient is light, not paintUse it as a blurred glow, a 4 to 8px mark, a 3px progress line or a media fill. Never as a button, a text background or a large flat panel.
  • One gradient per siteChoose one of the three and use it everywhere. Two gradients on one screen is never allowed.
  • Dark mode inverts, it doesn't recolourThe page becomes true black, cards become near-black greys, and primary turns white with black text. The gradient stays the same.

Typography

One family, thin to bold

Hanken Grotesk is used for everything: a clean, neutral grotesque with weights from 200 to 800. Weight does the work that a second typeface usually would. Display sizes are ultralight and tightly spaced, reading text is regular with generous line height, and small labels are semibold capitals with open letter spacing. The fallback stack is Helvetica Neue, Helvetica, then Arial.

Rules

  • Thin only when largeWeight 200 is for 40px and above, 300 for 24px and above. Anything smaller is 400 or heavier, so thin strokes never break up on low-quality screens.
  • Tight at the top, open at the bottomLetter spacing tightens as size grows (down to −0.035em on the name) and opens on small capitals (+0.1em).
  • Short linesParagraphs stop at about 62 characters. Headings balance across their lines rather than leaving one word alone.
  • Numbers line upFigures in stats, times and dates use tabular numerals so they don't shift when they change.
  • No bold paragraphsEmphasis inside running text is semibold at most, and rare.
Shape and space

Small corners, short buttons

Corners are tight and get only slightly rounder as surfaces get bigger: 6px on fields, 10px on cards and 14px on the largest tiles and bands. Buttons and chips are the only round-ended shapes, and they are kept short (34 to 38px) so they read as compact controls rather than capsules. An invisible hit area brings every control up to a 44px touch target.

Materials

Four levels of frosted glass

A material is a translucent fill, a background blur and a 1px hairline edge. The four levels differ in how much of what's behind them shows through: thin lets the most through, thick the least. They only read as glass when there is colour or texture behind them, so every specimen here sits on the dot grid and the gradient glow. Switch on "Reduce transparency" at the top to see the solid fallback.

Backdrops

Glass needs something to blur. A flat colour gives a flat result; the three backdrops below are what make it work.

Rules

  • One layer of glassNever put a material inside another material. Blurring something already blurred looks muddy, and inner corners clash with outer ones. Tint the outer surface instead.
  • Nothing moving insideAnimated content repaints the blur on every frame. Anything that moves sits on a solid surface.
  • Glass is optionalWith reduced transparency or increased contrast turned on, or in a browser without blur, every material becomes a solid surface and the washes disappear. Nothing depends on the effect to be readable.
Section rhythm

Dots mark every other section

A fine grid of dots, 1px each and 22px apart, runs edge to edge behind alternating sections. It tells a visitor where one section ends and the next begins without adding another colour, and it gives frosted surfaces a texture to blur. This page uses the same rhythm: this section, Principles, Typography, Showcase tiles, Glass budget and Open decisions all sit on the grid.

The building blocks

Each component is shown with Dave's real content where it exists. For each one: what it's for, what it's made of, and how it behaves.

Buttons and links

Use

Primary for the one main action on a screen. Pill links for a few equal, lighter destinations, such as profile links. Ghost for going back. Text links for "see more" inside a section.

Anatomy

Label in weight 500, 14.5px (13.5px small). 38px tall, or 34px small, with fully round ends and 18px side padding. Optional arrow after text links.

States

Hover darkens primary slightly. Pressed nudges 1px down. Disabled drops to 42% opacity and shows a not-allowed cursor. Focus shows a 2px gradient-colour ring.

Testimonial Sample quotes

Use

Social proof: one client quote at a time, with a way to read more.

Anatomy

A thick-glass card on the dot grid, at most 640px wide. A capital label, the quote in light italic (25px), the client's name and company, dot controls and a "Read more" text link.

Behaviour

Moves to the next quote every 6 seconds with a short fade. Pauses on hover or keyboard focus. Stops rotating with reduced motion. The active dot stretches into a short black bar.

Logo grid Lettered placeholders

Use

Shows the software Dave works in, as reassurance rather than navigation.

Anatomy

A fluid grid of square-ish tiles, at least 112px wide (three columns on phones). Each tile has a hairline border, the logo, and the tool's name in small text below.

Behaviour

Logos are greyscale at rest and turn full colour on hover, or on tap on touch screens (one at a time). Tiles are not links. Real logos follow each brand's guidelines.

Fields and chips

Use

Text fields for names, emails and messages. Chips for picking one or more options from a short, known list.

Anatomy

Fields: a semibold label above a 48px box with 6px corners and a hairline border. Chips: 36px, round-ended, hairline border; a tick appears when selected.

States

Focus replaces the border with a 2px ring. Errors turn the border red and add a plain message underneath saying how to fix it. Selected chips fill black with white text.

Showcase tiles

Previews of the work

A showcase tile introduces a collection (documents, videos or printed pieces) and lets a visitor open one item straight away. Text sits on the left: a title (26px, weight 400), one line of description and a ghost "View all" button. A live preview of the collection sits on the right and drops below the text when the tile is narrower than about 620px. Tiles are solid (white, or near-black in dark mode) with a 1.5px outline and 14px corners, because their previews move and glass would repaint on every frame.

Booking flow

The dark band is the destination

Every page ends on the same dark band: a light heading, one supporting line and a white card with a four-step request form (when, about you, your need, confirm). It's the only dark section on a page, so it reads as the place everything leads to. The card is solid rather than glass because forms need the clearest possible background. Glass budget

About six blurred elements per page

Blur costs processing power, and most visitors will be on phones. Keep each page to about six blurred elements. A material goes where it has colour or texture behind it and nothing moving inside it.

Open decisions

Still to confirm

The system is settled apart from these choices. A yes or an alternative on each is enough to start building components.

Gradient

Sky (cyan to blue), Sunset (orange to pink) or Mint (green to sky blue). Try each with the switch at the top. If Dave already has a brand colour, it can become one end of the gradient.

Display weight

Weight 200 for the largest type looks most refined. Weight 300 is sturdier on older or low-quality screens.

Portrait

A black-and-white cut-out suits the system best, with the gradient glow behind it. Full colour is the alternative.

Showcase outline

Keep the 1.5px dark outline on showcase tiles, or soften it to a hairline.

Dark mode

Fully designed here. Is it needed at launch, or can it follow later?
